    How a run goes

    Build a scenario, run it, pick a response

    01 · Build a scenario

    Draw the storm. Stack the conditions.

    Draw a tornado, derecho, thunderstorm, ice storm or blizzard path on the map. Add extreme heat, extreme cold or high wind, and grid faults. Set a start time.

    02 · Press Run

    One clock, one tick every four minutes

    The storm lands hits as it moves. Power lines are cut, bus lines close and buildings go dark.

    03 · Compare five policies

    Five policies on five copies

    Each response policy runs your scenario on its own copy of the campus through the peak, with the same storm and the same supply.

    04 · Adopt the best

    The live campus follows it

    Policies are ranked by people in a lit shelter. Adopt the best and the live campus switches to it.

    Agents

    Decisions respond to estimated occupancy

    Energy agent

    Allocates scarce power

    Each tick it sheds demand according to the chosen policy, excluding critical-priority buildings from deliberate shedding. The people-per-kW policy uses occupancy estimates to compare how many people each kilowatt serves.

    Transit agent

    Models relocation to powered buildings

    When a building goes dark, its modeled occupants move to the nearest eligible powered building reachable along open roads. If none is reachable, they remain in place. Headcounts are schedule-derived estimates; relocation is illustrative.

    Hard limit

    No invented power

    Supply on a feed is a ceiling. Agents shed, reroute and relocate. They cannot create kilowatts that the feed does not have.

    Five policies

    Compare them all before you commit

    Priority tiers

    Shed the lowest priority tier first. Critical care is never shed.

    Protect residential

    Keep dorms powered. Academic and commons buildings go dark first.

    Protect classes

    Keep classrooms and libraries powered. Residence halls go dark first.

    Most people per kW

    Shed the buildings that serve the fewest people per kilowatt first.

    Ration evenly

    No load shedding. Every building on the feed gets the same share.

    Each policy runs your scenario on its own copy of the campus through the peak, with the same agents, the same storm and the same supply. They are ranked by people in a lit shelter. The live campus does not change until you adopt one. The shed orders pictured use the simplified heat-wave preview model above.

    Optional layers

    Sponsor tech, switched on when it is there

    Fetch.ai

    Intent becomes simulator action

    Reach the running PARALLEL Coordinator through ASI:One. Ask “an ice storm hit North Campus, what should we do?” to execute simulator tools. Three planning functions provide tested energy-policy options, transit guidance and repair priorities. Reply “adopt 1” to apply an option.

    SpacetimeDB

    Live multiplayer

    Open two windows and they share one campus. An “N online” badge shows who else is in it.

    Language models

    Grok first, Claude as fallback

    Briefings and text try Grok first and fall back to Claude, then to deterministic text, so the app always has words. Voice commands use Grok.

    This is a teaching model, not a forecast of the real grid. Use it to practise a decision, not to predict an outage.

    • RealBuilding locations, feed topology, GTFS bus routes, the class schedule, FEMA and NOAA hazard frequencies, and live NWS weather.
    • ScaledKilowatts. Power figures are a scaled demo model, not metered campus data.
    • RulesStorm effects. What a storm does to lines, buses and buildings follows written rules.
